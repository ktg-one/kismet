# Kismet Finance Group - Copy & Voice Guide

## The "John and Jenny" Test

Every word on the site is filtered through:

> "Would a normal Australian say this at the kitchen table?"

John and Jenny are a typical suburban Australian couple. Mid-30s to 50s. Working. Paying a mortgage or thinking about buying. Not finance professionals. Not private-wealth clients. Just people who want to make a good decision on what is likely their largest financial commitment.

If the copy sounds like it belongs in a wealth management brochure, a private bank prospectus, or an agency pitch deck - it fails the test.

**Operator voice**: Kismet speaks like the knowledgeable friend in the industry. Direct, clear, capable. Not clever. Not jargony. Not modest to the point of invisible.

## Hard Rules (Non-Negotiable)

### Em-Dashes and En-Dashes - NEVER USE

**No em-dashes (--) or en-dashes (-) anywhere on the site. Ever.**

This is the single most enforced rule. Em-dashes are an AI writing tell that kills credibility instantly. Use commas, periods, or rewrite the sentence.

```
WRONG: Kismet helps you see the bigger picture -- and then does something about it.
WRONG: This is a major financial commitment -- one that deserves a clear head.
RIGHT: Kismet helps you see the bigger picture. Then does something about it.
RIGHT: This is a major financial commitment. It deserves a clear head.
```

### No Regulated Advice Territory

Never write in a way that sounds like Kismet is giving personal financial, credit, investment, or tax advice. Kismet does not hold AFSL or ACL.

**Forbidden patterns:**
- "You should..." (regulated advice territory)
- "We recommend you invest in..."
- "The right loan for you is..."
- Any language that implies Kismet is making a personal recommendation on financial products

**Acceptable capability language:**
- "We help you compare..."
- "We find the right fit..."
- "We look at the full picture..."
- "We connect you with..."

## Banned Words and Phrases

### Absolute bans (AI-tells or jargon)
- `delve` - classic AI writing tell
- `leverage` (as a verb, e.g. "leverage our network") - business-speak AI tell
- `unlock` - overused, meaningless
- `holistic` - hollow wellness-speak in finance context
- `synergy` - generic management consulting noise
- `ecosystem` - tech-bro language, not operator language
- `bespoke` - class-signalling word that fails John and Jenny test
- `tailored` - hollow finance-speak
- `curated` - belongs in a boutique retail context, not mortgage broking
- `wealth` - signals private wealth territory, excludes John and Jenny
- `high net worth` - explicitly excluded audience signal
- `boutique` - wrong register for Kismet's actual audience

### Avoid (flag for review)
- Amplify Solutions Group phrasing (studied as competitor, NOT to be copied)
- "Retain Full Control" (flagged as Amplify-adjacent)
- "tailored to your circumstances" (Amplify-adjacent, also feels like a disclaimer)
- "Begin the Conversation" (was replaced with "Book a call")
- "Get Started" (generic - use "Book a call")
- "Learn More" (generic - use specific action language)
- "Book a Consultation" (too formal / premium-signals wrong register)

## Voice Principles

### Capability voice, not liability voice

The site leads with what Kismet does, not what it cannot do. Compliance language is quarantined to:
1. The Boundaries section on `/how-we-work`
2. The `ComplianceLine` component (appears at bottom of every Insights article)

Capability voice example:
```
WRONG (liability-first): "We're not financial advisers, but we can help you..."
RIGHT (capability-first): "We look across roughly 70 lenders to find the fit that works for you."
```

### Three-beat home positioning

The hero explicitly sets three beats that unfold across the site:
1. See the bigger picture (BiggerPicture section)
2. Connect with the right people (StrategicPathways section)
3. Make the whole process feel clearer (Approach/How We Work section)

When writing home page copy, support these beats - do not introduce a fourth framing.

### Tone calibration

- **Confident, not boastful** - Kismet earns credibility by how it explains things, not by claiming to be best
- **Direct, not blunt** - Get to the point; don't pad or hedge; but never cold
- **Warm, not chatty** - Approachable but not over-familiar; this is a significant financial conversation
- **Smart, not clever** - Insight over wordplay; John and Jenny don't have time for clever

## Headings and Subheadings

- Short, active, declarative
- Avoid question-form headings ("Are you ready for...?") - they feel manipulative
- Avoid gerund stacks ("Connecting, coordinating, delivering") - they feel breathless
- One strong idea per heading, then the body copy explains

## What Was Tried and Rejected

- **Agency cleverness** - "Begin the Conversation" (too boutique, replaced with "Book a call")
- **Boutique copywriter voice** - drifted toward clever, pulled back to operator
- **Amplify-influenced outcomes language** - studied and explicitly kept OUT of Kismet voice
- **Liability-first framing** - "we're not X but..." leads killed. Lead with capability.
- **Private-wealth register** - any word that signals exclusivity or high-end wealth management
