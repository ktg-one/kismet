# Copy Voice Guide

## The one rule

If John and Jenny would not say it at the kitchen table, it does not go on the site.

John and Jenny are normal Australians. They earn decent money. They own a home, have some super. They are not poor and they are not wealthy. They are smart, but they are not finance people. They are tired of being talked over by banks, brokers and advisers.

Every sentence is written for them.

## Voice in one line

Plain English, Australian, premium, clear, human, confident.

## What we sound like

- A trusted friend who happens to know finance.
- Calm. Direct. Confident. Not loud.
- Aware that the reader has been spoken at before, and intentionally not doing that.
- Specific where it matters (numbers, partners, panel size). Vague language is a credibility leak.
- Short sentences. Real words.

## What we never sound like

- A bank.
- A boutique advisory pitch deck.
- An LinkedIn-bro post.
- A finance influencer.
- A copywriter showing off.
- A lawyer hiding behind disclaimers.

## Hard rules

### 1. No em-dashes or en-dashes anywhere. Ever.

This is non-negotiable. Em-dashes and en-dashes are an AI tell that kills credibility. Use periods, commas or parentheses. Number ranges write out the word "to" (e.g. "5 to 10"), never the dash form. Hyphens for compound words ("10-Minute", "day-to-day") are fine.

### 2. Never give regulated financial advice.

Kismet is an introducer, not a licensed advisor. No "you should...". General observations only. Specifics get referred to the licensed specialist in the network.

### 3. Lead with capability, not disclaimer.

Compliance facts live in the Boundaries section on the Approach page, in the ComplianceLine component, and in the footer. Everywhere else, the voice is confident and capability-led. We do not pre-apologise for being an introducer in the middle of marketing copy.

### 4. The 10:1 value ratio.

Every prospect-facing message delivers 10x value for every 1 ask. Insights, framing, stats, story. A "touch base" with no value is forbidden.

## Words and phrases to use

- "We help you see..."
- "We bring the right people into the room."
- "We pull it together."
- "We keep the strategy moving."
- "Plain English."
- "John and Jenny" (internally only).
- "Normal Australians", "everyday Australians".
- "The bigger picture."
- "Connected" (vs. "patched together").
- "Real options."
- "On the same page."
- "Calm pace."
- "Book a call."

## Words and phrases to avoid

These have shown up across the build and been killed for being either jargon, defensive, or agency-clever:

- "Strategic financial coordination" (as a category descriptor in body copy. Acceptable in formal compliance attribution only.)
- "Begin the conversation" (replaced with "Book a call").
- "Bespoke", "tailored", "curated".
- "Wealth", "wealthy few", "high net worth", "private wealth".
- "Sit in the room while they do the regulated work" (liability-voice, killed).
- "Not us in their seat" (defensive, killed).
- "We sit in the middle" (passive, replaced with active framings like "we run the play across", "we pull it together").
- "We are an introducer" (frame as coordinator instead).
- "Operator notes" (finance-bro framing, killed).
- "Holistic", "synergy", "leverage" (as a verb), "ecosystem".
- "Architects", "blueprints", "harmony", "in harmony" (writerly).
- "Amidst" (use "in" or rephrase).
- "Strategic move", "strategic pathways" (in body copy. "Strategy" alone is fine.)
- "It pays to..." (sales-pitch tone).
- "You should..." (regulated advice).
- "Guaranteed", "guarantee", "promise", "ensure" (re. financial outcomes).

## John and Jenny readability test

Before any copy ships, run it past these:

1. Could I say this sentence out loud to my mum without sounding like a brochure?
2. Is there a shorter, simpler word for any word in this sentence?
3. If I deleted this sentence, would the page lose anything important?
4. Am I describing what we DO, or am I describing how clever we are at doing it?
5. Does this sound like a real person, or like an agency wrote it?

## Examples: good vs bad

### Hero sub

Bad: "For everyday Australians who already have something built but feel like the moves aren't lining up. We drive the strategy, open the right doors, and make sure the work you started actually finishes."
(Operator-y. "Drive the strategy" and "open the right doors" sound like an agency pitch.)

Good: "We help everyday Australians see the bigger picture, connect with the right people, and make the whole process feel clearer."
(Three plain beats. Reads on first scan. John and Jenny recognise themselves.)

### Bigger Picture intro

Bad: "Real outcomes are rarely about one product. They come from how the parts of your financial life fit together. Lending, structure, professionals, timing. We sit in the middle of all of it."
(Abstract. "Real outcomes". "Sit in the middle" is passive.)

Good: "Most of the time, getting ahead isn't about one product. It's about how lending, property, structure and timing all fit together. That's the part most people never see. We pull it together with you."
(Conversational. Real verbs. The reader feels seen in "the part most people never see".)

### Network credentials disclaimer

Bad: "We do not hold our own credit licence. The regulated work is done by the licensed brokers in the AFG network we introduce you to."
(Reads like a paralegal wrote it. Apologetic tone.)

Good: "Kismet operates as an authorised representative within the Home Loan Solutions / AFG network. The brokers in our network hold their own credentials. We bring the strategy and the relationships, they bring the lender access."
(Same compliance facts, framed as a partnership. Calm and confident, not defensive.)

### CTA label

Bad: "Begin the Conversation."
(Slightly precious. Half-step abstract.)

Good: "Book a call."
(Specific. Action. John and Jenny know exactly what happens when they click.)

## When in doubt

Read it out loud. If it sounds like a real person talking, ship it. If it sounds like a brochure, rewrite it.
